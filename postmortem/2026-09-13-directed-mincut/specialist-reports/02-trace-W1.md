## 6. Top 3 lessons from this window

1. The whole reduction (I1–I3) was in hand by 15:10:37 (25 minutes into the run) and the author correctly named the missing primitive; every later step *strengthened* the primitive (packing → dynamic exact oracle → dual revisions) instead of weakening it. A prompt-level rule is needed: after a conditional reduction, write the weakest sufficient object (here: one random tree whose marginals are ≤αc/λ) and enumerate ways to reach it that avoid the explicit object (iterative averaging toward a target marginal vector, flow-based moves from the current tree), before investing in constructing the strong object.
2. Ideas that surfaced only in reasoning summaries (Markov chains on arborescences 15:17:04, matroid intersection 15:17:19) never reached a record and were lost; only what a subagent put in its final message survived. Subagent tasks should require a short "considered but not developed" list, and the root should be asked to cross-check textbook tools (arborescence polytope, Edmonds' disjoint branchings, flow rounding) against the primitive before opening data-structure branches.
3. About 40% of the root's time went to writing/validating records and a further ~12% to verifying its own mostly negative lemmas (L009, L010, L020, L021 all within 27 minutes), with two author-initiated "final summary" turn ends and a mid-run prompt edit that forced a full rewrite of all nodes. Negative lemmas against the author's own sub-proposals should be recorded tersely and verified later; INITIAL_PROMPT.md must not be edited while a turn runs; and turn boundaries should not require a deliverable-style summary plus a full re-read.
