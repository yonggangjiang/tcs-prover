### General objections

1. Unit of account. Every share is in API-list-price 'bill units' (r = 0.1 or 0.25, output 8×), but the constraint that actually ended the run was a Pro weekly quota whose weighting of cached/uncached/output/tier is unknown; the proposal says so, and the usedPercent least-squares fit is from integer-rounded readings with ±1 jitter (the trajectory oscillates 27→26→27 repeatedly), so the '56/28/16' split should not be used for anything quantitative. 2. The simulator does not reproduce the run: S0 gives C = 280M, U = 10.5M, 37 compactions and 130k mean context against the recorded 258.9M / 9.05M / 30 / 120k, so every scenario fraction (0.822, 0.748, 0.650 ...) carries roughly 8–16% model error before any assumption about behaviour; the 'cheap re-anchor' (S3) assumes a 3 KB overview replaces all ritual reads, while C2a itself notes the current `overview` prints ≈10 KB. 3. The hidden-compaction cost (+10.8%/+7.1%) assumes a 12–28k-token summary billed as output at 8× plus a full-context read; nothing in the transcript records it, compaction is a 'remote compact task' of unknown billing, and both 'a 120k ceiling without a re-anchor is a net loss (1.061)' and D's '+4–6%' rest entirely on it (without it the 120k ceiling is 0.881). 4. Savings are stated per item in C (25–35%, 18–23%, 10–15%, 5–10%, 3%, 2–5%, 3–8% ...) and sum to well over 70%; only D's bottom-up figure (0.53) avoids additive double counting, and it rests on assumed call counts (1,200–1,500) and a 57k mean context that no simulator scenario produced jointly (B4′ reaches 55k context but keeps all 2,228 calls). Overlaps are real: 119 of the 408 verification calls are 'report to root' and 63 are node reads, claimed again by C4a and C2a; INITIAL_PROMPT reads are claimed by C2a, C2b and C3; ritual reads by C2a and C3. 5. Removing chatter or narration calls removes whole-call tokens in the attribution tables, but the reasoning done inside those calls (1,171 + 754 reasoning items in the two message classes) would migrate to other calls, so C4a's '−506 calls ≈ 61M ≈ 23% of tokens' is an upper bound, not an expectation. 6. Injected-text shares (29.5%/36.1%, 44.5% of cached) are attributions of re-sends until the next compaction, not savings; the proposal's own sawtooth argument (E.6) says removing them mostly delays compactions, yet section A's table presents them beside call-count levers as if comparable. Token counts for injected text use 3.6 bytes/token; LaTeX-heavy markdown likely tokenizes nearer 3, so those shares are probably understated by 15–20% (direction favours the argument, but the numbers are estimates). 7. The tier saving (up to 33%/50%) is a docstring claim; D's headline 0.35/0.28 depends on it, and even the same-tier 0.53 depends on C1a's --ignore-user-config fix behaving as assumed. 8. Several numbers in the synthesis are not produced by any script under eff/ and should be regenerated or dropped before the document is used: the ritual '~9–10% of the bill' (scripts give 8–11% for a 3-min window, 16–26% for the 12-min window the sentence cites), 'ROOT ... 21 wait polls at ~190k' (3 on ROOT at 123–127k; mean 138k), '64 whole-set AUDITS cats' (11–13), 'A055 ×3 = 48 calls, 8.3M' (81 calls / 12.9M; 48/8.3M is the third pass alone), and the '13 IDs ≥2×' / '18 clean, 31 wording, 0 substantive' tallies, which the cited tables_duplicates.txt does not contain. 9. Verification-class attribution depends on a keyword classifier (46 tasks) or a hand list (49); the two disagree on individual tasks (e.g. #118 'Both audits pass' is classified new-math), so the 19.7–21.9% band is classifier spread, not measurement uncertainty. 10. Auditor cost is inferred from a 27→29 quota step that also contains the first resumed author call; the second batch shows only 63→64, so '≈2% per batch' is a one-sample upper estimate.

### General objections

1. The two largest levers (C4a task-scoped subagents = context reset per task; C3 150k ceiling = compactions 30→48) both reduce what an agent holds in context when it thinks, which is the mechanism the postmortem blames (P2 unrecorded reasoning lost at compaction; C2/C5 building on misremembered hypotheses). Every mitigation offered (PLAN checkpoint, 2 KB packets, overview re-anchor) is an untested S-change, and the run's best mathematics came from persistent threads exchanging interim messages in their first 40 minutes; the proposal reads that as support for short tasks, but it equally supports the regime it removes. The savings should be booked only after F2/F3 show no loss in the §7 ledger criterion.
2. Verification is mislabeled as fruitless (K9, K10). 27 of 49 verification results carried corrections, several missing hypotheses or missed proof cases; the postmortem's 'no false lemma' describes the records after those fixes and lists rigor under 'what to keep'. C4b (one pass, no web) and C1b (verifiers at xhigh, i.e. halved reasoning) rest on '0 substantive errors', which the results do not support. The duplicate-verification evidence (13 IDs, A055 x3 = 48 calls/8.3M) is not in the cited file and is wrong for A055 (different sections, one write, 48 calls is one task).
3. Static attributions are presented next to dynamic ones and will be double-booked: injected text 29.5% (K5), rituals 39%/45% and 9–10% (K8), low-output calls 22.5% (K11) are accounting identities or window artifacts; the proposal's own simulator says read discipline alone is worth 0–8%. The 12-minute ritual window covers roughly a quarter to a third of active time, so 39% of commands is barely above the base rate.
4. Subagent tasks were already short (median 10 calls, p90 25, 1 of 125 above 40); the runner call/interaction caps in C4a bind nothing and save nothing. The whole C4a saving is the fresh-reader reset, and the proposal should own that trade explicitly instead of presenting caps as the mechanism. The interim messages' texts are not in the transcript, so 'chatter' is asserted from counts alone.
5. The hidden-compaction estimate treats the retained developer/user messages (skills 9.1 KB, plugins 5.2 KB, initial message 6.7 KB, each resume prompt 7.2 KB, every steer) as summary; the rollout shows these explicitly, so the 556k figure is overstated by roughly a third to a half and the '120k is a net loss' ordering is fragile. Conversely the proposal misses the cheapest strength-neutral saving it exposes: the runner's own resume template re-pastes the full author prompt as a permanent user message (two copies after two resumes), and the skills/plugins blocks add ~3.9k tokens to every call; `include_apps_instructions=false`, `skills.include_instructions=false` and a resume template that does not embed {original_prompt} remove ~2% with no effect on what the author needs.
6. The quota, not the bill, ended the run. My fit of usedPercent against token classes (cached 0.24, uncached 1.47, output 12.6 %/M; ≈98.5% of the quota reconstructed) confirms cached context is ~62% of the quota, so context reduction is the right target, but it also means the one lever that touches nothing the model sees (standard tier, C1a) is unmeasured and ranked second; F1 should run before any of C3/C4 is adopted, and the D bundle should be reported in quota-% terms rather than in r-variant bill units.
7. INITIAL_PROMPT.md retention holds only for the root (rollout-proven); the working tree's 'never re-read INITIAL_PROMPT.md' rule is safe for the root and for VERIFY/LOOKUP packets, but any subagent that must write records in the prescribed format, and any segment after the file is edited, needs the current text delivered in the packet or steer, not assumed from the prefix.
