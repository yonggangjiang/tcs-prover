Let $f:\S^2\to \S^2$ be a branched cover, $V(f)$ be the set of its critical values,  and $A, B\subset \S^2$ be  finite sets.  \begin{enumerate} \item If $f(A)\cup V(f)\subset B$, then every complex structure on $(\S^2, B)$ can be pulled back to a complex structure on $(\S^2, A)$, and this induces the map  $f^*:\Teich(B)\to \Teich(A)$.  \item If $A\subset B$, then forgetting the points $B\backslash A$ induces the map $i_*:\Teich(B)\to \Teich(A)$.   \end{enumerate} If $f(A)\cup V(f)\cup A\subset B,$ then the deformation space is defined by $$\D_f:=\{\tau\in \Teich(B): f^*(\tau)=i_*(\tau)\}.$$ Consider a quadratic rational map $f$ with critical points $c_1$ and $c_2$. Assume that  $c_1$ belongs to a cycle of length $4$, and the critical value $v=f(c_2)$ does not belong to the cycle. By taking  $$A=\{c_1, f(c_1), f^{\circ 2}(c_1), f^{\circ 3}(c_1)\}, \quad B=A\cup \{v\},$$ we get a deformation space $\D_f\subset \Teich(B)$. Its image in the moduli space is the set of conjugacy classes of quadratic rational maps with the properties described above.  Explicitly describe a simple closed curve $\gamma\subset (\S^2, B)$ such that a power of the Dehn twist around this curve preserves the deformation space, and its action on the set of connected components of $\D_f$ has an infinite orbit.
